Frequently Asked Questions

How is a monthly auto loan payment calculated?
Monthly car loan payments are calculated using standard amortization: M = P [ i(1 + i)^n ] / [ (1 + i)^n – 1 ], where P is the total financed vehicle price minus down payment and trade-in value, i is the monthly interest rate (annual APR divided by 12), and n is the total number of loan months.
What is the recommended car loan term?
Financial advisors generally recommend loan terms between 36 and 60 months. While 72- or 84-month auto loans lower monthly payments, they substantially increase total interest charges and raise the risk of becoming upside down.
How does a trade-in value affect auto loan financing?
Your trade-in equity acts as an immediate down payment, directly reducing the net financed amount. In many jurisdictions, trading in your existing vehicle also reduces the taxable purchase price, saving money on sales tax.
How much should I put down on a car loan?
The traditional benchmark is 20% down for new cars and 10% for used cars. A healthy down payment helps offset immediate depreciation, lowers monthly payments, qualifies you for better APR tiers, and avoids negative equity.
