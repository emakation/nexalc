Financial & Everyday Math Glossary

Mortgages & Lending

Amortization
Loans · Mortgages
The scheduled process of paying off a debt over time through fixed, regular installments. In the early years of a standard amortizing mortgage, most of each payment covers interest; as the balance drops, an increasing share goes toward reducing the loan principal.
Run an amortization schedule in the Mortgage Calculator →
Principal
Loans · Mortgages
The original sum of money borrowed (or the unpaid portion of that amount still remaining), excluding interest, taxes, and fees. On a $400,000 home purchased with an $80,000 down payment, your starting loan principal is $320,000.
See principal vs. interest split in the Mortgage Calculator →
APR (Annual Percentage Rate)
Loans · Credit
The yearly cost of borrowing expressed as a percentage, combining the base interest rate with lender origination fees, discount points, and certain closing costs. Comparing APRs across lenders gives a more accurate picture of total borrowing cost than comparing headline interest rates alone.
Compare loan rates with the Mortgage Calculator →
Escrow
Mortgages · Housing
A dedicated holding account managed by a mortgage servicer to collect monthly portions of your annual property taxes and homeowners insurance alongside your principal and interest payment, then disburse those bills automatically when due.
Estimate taxes & insurance in the Mortgage Calculator →
PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)
Mortgages · Down Payment
Insurance required on most conventional mortgages when a buyer puts down less than 20% of the purchase price (an 80%+ loan-to-value ratio). PMI protects the lender—not the borrower—against default and typically adds 0.5% to 1.5% of the loan amount per year until equity reaches 20%.
Model PMI costs in the Mortgage Calculator →
Down Payment
Real Estate · Financing
The upfront cash portion of a purchase paid out of pocket at closing, reducing the amount you must finance. A larger down payment lowers both your monthly payment and total interest paid over the life of the loan, and reaching 20% eliminates PMI on conventional mortgages.
Test down payment scenarios in the Mortgage Calculator →
Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)
Underwriting · Budgeting
Your total monthly debt obligations divided by your gross monthly income, expressed as a percentage. Under the classic 28/36 guideline, housing payments stay at or below 28% of gross income (front-end DTI) and all debts combined stay at or below 36% (back-end DTI).
Read our monthly mortgage payment & DTI guide →
Compound Interest
Savings · Debt
Interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from prior periods. Compounding frequency—daily, monthly, or semi-annually (as legally mandated for fixed-rate mortgages in Canada)—directly affects the effective interest rate you earn or pay.

Percentages & Everyday Pricing

Percentage Change
Math · Analytics
The relative increase or decrease between an original baseline value and a new value, calculated as ((New Value − Old Value) ÷ |Old Value|) × 100. Because it always divides by the starting value, a 25% drop followed by a 25% gain does not return you to the original number.
Solve increases and decreases with the Percentage Calculator →
Percentage Points
Rates · Statistics
The simple arithmetic difference between two percentages. If a mortgage rate rises from 5% to 6%, that is an increase of 1 percentage point—but a 20% relative percentage increase (1 ÷ 5 = 0.20). Distinguishing the two prevents misreading rate headlines and tax changes.
Compare percentages in the Percentage Calculator →
Markup vs. Margin
Retail · Business Math
Markup measures profit as a percentage of cost ((Price − Cost) ÷ Cost), while gross margin measures profit as a percentage of selling price ((Price − Cost) ÷ Price). An item bought for $50 and sold for $100 carries a 100% markup but a 50% margin.
Check pricing ratios with the Percentage Calculator →
Discount Rate (Percent Off)
Shopping · Budgeting
The percentage reduction applied to a product's original list price. To find the final checkout price quickly, multiply the original price by (1 − Discount Decimal)—for example, 30% off $80 equals $80 × 0.70 = $56, saving $24.

Currency & Gratuity

Mid-Market Exchange Rate
Foreign Exchange · Travel
The midpoint between the wholesale buy (bid) and sell (ask) prices for two currencies traded on global interbank markets. It is the baseline reference rate without retail bank markups and is the benchmark used to judge how fair a travel exchange or wire quote really is.
Check live rates in the Currency Converter →
Exchange Rate Spread
Foreign Exchange · Fees
The gap between the mid-market exchange rate and the rate a bank, card issuer, or airport kiosk actually offers you. Even services advertising "zero commission" frequently embed a 3% to 10% spread inside the conversion rate.
Compare your quote against the Currency Converter →
Gratuity (Tip)
Dining · Service
A voluntary percentage added to a service bill—most commonly 15% to 20% in US full-service dining—calculated on the pre-tax subtotal. Some venues add an automatic service charge for large groups, so always inspect the receipt before calculating an extra tip.

Health & Calendar Math

BMI (Body Mass Index)
Health · Screening
A standardized screening index defined by the World Health Organization as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²). While useful for population-level risk bands (underweight, healthy, overweight, obese), BMI does not directly distinguish between lean muscle mass and body fat.
Calculate your score in the BMI Calculator →
Body Fat Percentage
Health · Body Composition
The proportion of total body weight made up of adipose (fat) tissue rather than bone, muscle, organs, and water. Because muscle is denser than fat, two adults with identical BMI scores can have substantially different body fat percentages and metabolic risk profiles.
Estimate BMI and body composition in the BMI Calculator →
Chronological Age & Leap Year
Calendar · Time
Chronological age measures the exact elapsed calendar time from your birth date to today in years, months, and days. Under the Gregorian calendar, a leap day (February 29) is added every year divisible by 4—except century years unless also divisible by 400—keeping calendar years aligned with Earth's 365.2425-day solar orbit.
